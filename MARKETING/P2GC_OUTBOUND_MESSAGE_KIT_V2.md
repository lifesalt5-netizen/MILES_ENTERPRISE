# P2GC Outbound Message Kit V2

Effective: 2026-08-23
Governing rule: every personalized fact must be verified before sending. Bracketed fields are data requirements, not permission to fabricate.

## Sequence A — GSA Zero-Sales Diagnostic

### Touch 1 — observation
Subject options: your GSA contract | quick GSA question | [Company] + GSA

[First name] — I was reviewing [Company]’s federal position and saw [verified GSA/vehicle sales condition].

That usually warrants a closer look at agency alignment, offer positioning, buyer visibility, vehicle usage, and the opportunity path behind the contract.

We can run a GSA Zero-Sales Diagnostic and show which of those appears to be limiting traction for [Company].

Would it be useful if I sent the findings?

### Touch 2 — consequence
Subject: re: [Company] + GSA

[First name] — the reason I reached out is that holding a Schedule without a defined activation path can turn the vehicle into shelfware.

For [Company], we would look specifically at [one verified dimension: agency demand / category alignment / eBuy relevance / incumbent activity / pricing-position signal].

If there is no realistic gap to fix, we will say that too.

Worth sending the diagnostic?

### Touch 3 — proof/method
Subject: what we check

The diagnostic is not another generic GSA review. We compare the contract and capabilities against actual buying behavior, target agencies, relevant acquisition paths, and the company’s current position.

The output is a short prioritized action path — not a list of everything a contractor could theoretically do.

Want me to run it for [Company]?

### Touch 4 — close loop
Subject: close the loop?

[First name] — should I close this out, or would a GSA Zero-Sales Diagnostic be useful for [Company]?

If it is useful, I can send the findings first and you can decide whether a conversation makes sense.

## Sequence B — Federal Revenue Gap Analysis

### Touch 1 — observation
Subject options: [Company] federal revenue gap | federal position for [Company] | a quick finding

[First name] — I was reviewing [Company] against government buying activity for [verified capability/category] and found [verified gap or signal].

That may indicate the company is capable of competing but is not yet aligned to the most realistic buyers, acquisition paths, vehicles, or teaming options.

We can map that in a Federal Revenue Gap Analysis and show the highest-priority path first.

Should I send you what we found?

### Touch 2 — specificity
Subject: re: federal position for [Company]

The analysis is designed to answer four practical questions:

1. Who is actually buying what [Company] sells?
2. Which route is realistic now — prime, subcontract, vehicle, SLED, or hybrid?
3. What is blocking access or conversion?
4. What should be worked first?

If useful, I can send the initial findings for [Company].

### Touch 3 — anti-volume positioning
Subject: not another opportunity list

[First name] — we do not start by flooding companies with solicitations.

We start by determining where the company can realistically compete, then use opportunity and buyer intelligence inside that pathway.

That is the gap I wanted to validate for [Company]. Want me to send it?

### Touch 4 — close loop
Subject: close this out?

Should I close the file on [Company], or send the Federal Revenue Gap findings first?

No obligation either way.

For each relevant path we want to know whether [Company] should prime, team, position early, or pass based on verified fit and access.

Want the findings?

[First name] — I can close this out, or send the Recompete & Vehicle Growth Scan for [Company] first.

Which is more useful?

## Reply routing

Qualified positive intents: INTERESTED, MEETING, PRICING, REFERRAL.

When a prospect replies positively:

1. Preserve the exact prospect/segment/offer attribution.
2. Respond around the specific finding that generated the reply.
3. Do not restart a generic company pitch.
4. Route to the diagnostic or meeting path.
5. Record booked meeting, show, proposal, close, and revenue outcome so campaign selection is tied to revenue.

## Testing order

1. Test the offer/pain pairing before cosmetic copy changes.
2. Test one first-touch hook at a time.
3. Keep the winning body stable while testing subject lines.
4. Judge winners by qualified replies, meetings, and revenue — not opens.
5. Retire messages that generate curiosity but not qualified downstream action.
