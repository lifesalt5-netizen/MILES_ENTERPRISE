## Monday, August 24 — LinkedIn

### Why a GSA Schedule can become shelfware even when the contractor is capable

Getting the Schedule is not the same as building a federal revenue path.

When a capable contractor holds a GSA Schedule but sees little or no traction, I would not start by telling them to bid more.

I would first check five things:

1. **Offer alignment** — Are the awarded offerings the things agencies are actually positioned to buy from this company?
2. **Buyer alignment** — Are the right agencies and buying offices being targeted?
3. **Opportunity fit** — Are pursuits truly aligned to capability, access, timing, and competitive position?
4. **Vehicle activation** — Is the Schedule being used as an acquisition path, or simply listed as a credential?
5. **Capture execution** — Is there a repeatable process connecting intelligence, outreach, qualification, positioning, and bid/no-bid decisions?

A contract vehicle creates access. It does not create demand, positioning, or capture discipline by itself.

That is the problem our **GSA Zero-Sales Diagnostic** is designed to isolate: where the revenue path is breaking down and what should be worked first.

## Wednesday, August 26 — LinkedIn

### Holding a vehicle is not the same as having an activation strategy

A government contract vehicle answers one important question:

**Can a buyer use this acquisition path to purchase from you?**

It does not automatically answer:

- Which agencies should be targeted first?
- Which buyers have a reason to care about your specific capability?
- Which opportunities deserve capture resources?
- Whether you should prime, team, or position early.
- Whether your public offering and market message match the way the government buys.

That distinction matters.

I think of the vehicle as infrastructure. The activation strategy connects that infrastructure to real demand.

At P2GC, we want the sequence to be:

**verify access → verify market alignment → identify the gap → prioritize the path → execute.**

Not:

**get vehicle → search everything → bid everything → hope something converts.**

### P2GC Intelligence Brief #1

**Subject:** Is your GSA contract actually positioned to sell?

A GSA Schedule can solve an access problem without solving a revenue problem.

When a Schedule produces little or no traction, the useful question is not simply, “What opportunities can we bid?” It is, “Where is the pathway breaking down?”

A practical review should separate at least five issues:

**1. Vehicle fit** — Are the awarded offerings aligned to the capabilities the company should actually take to government?

**2. Agency and buyer alignment** — Are you targeting buying organizations with a defensible reason to purchase what you sell?

**3. Opportunity fit** — Are the pursuits aligned to scope, timing, access, competitive position, and delivery capability?

**4. Prime versus team strategy** — Is direct pursuit realistic, or would a partner path provide better access and positioning?

**5. Capture execution** — Is there a repeatable process from intelligence through qualification, outreach, positioning, and bid/no-bid?

A vehicle should be part of a revenue system, not a credential that sits on a capability statement.

P2GC's **GSA Zero-Sales Diagnostic** is designed to identify the highest-priority gap before a contractor spends more time and money pursuing the wrong activity.
